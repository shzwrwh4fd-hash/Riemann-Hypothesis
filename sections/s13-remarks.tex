\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:remarks}

\subsection{Scope}
The theorems proved here concern the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet $L$-functions. The method of
\cref{sec:hosts,sec:stepanov} works with abelian host fields and with characters of their Galois
groups. In the formal development it is applied only to Dirichlet characters. Dedekind zeta functions,
Hecke $L$-functions and automorphic $L$-functions are not covered by the statements that have been
verified \cite{Sutherland2026}. The announcement \cite{Announcement2026} expects the method to extend
further.

\subsection{Weak forms of classical results}
Several arithmetic inputs are used in forms that are weaker than the corresponding classical theorems.
\begin{itemize}
\item The local parameters of \cref{thm:rectexists} satisfy a list of laws on a ``good'' class of
representations, without bijectivity and without compatibility with $L$- and $\eps$-factors of pairs.
\item The Galois representations of \cref{thm:row0b} have only part of the local--global compatibility
of \cite{BarnetLambEtAl2014}.
\item The vanishing statements of \cref{thm:kato} are rational, not integral.
\end{itemize}
These weaker forms are all the argument needs, and they shorten the dependency on the deepest parts of
the Langlands program. Conversely, several classical theorems are proved in the development in full,
or in variant forms (\cref{sec:literature}).

\subsection{Effectivity}
All arguments are effective in principle, but the constants are enormous. The hosts at scale $x$
involve Galois groups of order about $x^{2+8\eps}$ and auxiliary primes of size $\exp(x^{\eps/5})$, and
the error terms carry factors $\exp(x^{\eps})$. The proof gives no numerical zero-free region beyond
the conclusion of the theorems.

\subsection{Simplifications}
The formal proof is very long, and many of its choices are dictated by the convenience of
formalization rather than by mathematical necessity. The two central ideas are:
\begin{itemize}
\item that one-sided bounds for Riesz-weighted prime sums on windows suffice
(\cref{thm:landau-window,thm:cheb-windows});
\item that inert-prime counts in Frobenius classes can be bounded above by comparing divisibility and
slopes of lattices built from long chains of theta lifts (\cref{sec:stepanov}).
\end{itemize}
Both ideas are independent of most of the machinery used to verify the hypotheses in full generality.
We expect substantially shorter proofs of the individual inputs, as anticipated in
\cite{Announcement2026}.
